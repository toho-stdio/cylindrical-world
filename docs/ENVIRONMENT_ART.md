# Environment art pass

## Generated scene

`src/world.js` now builds the scene around the fixed 140 m-radius, 480 m cylindrical habitat and seed `731942`. The startup riverbend is art-directed around the opening camera: Riverbend Cottage at `x=-163, theta=.03`; a flowering tree at `(-166,.34)`; companion broadleaf at `(-158,-.31)`; planted riverbanks, grasses and a winding path in the foreground; and the stone crossing at `x=-140`. Groves and clearings alternate around the winding stream rather than using uniform tree scattering. The orchard has deliberately staggered, wider-spaced mixed fruit/broadleaf/blossom rows around `x=75..113`, with fruit accents, additional cottage clusters and meadows along the route. The Ring Aqueduct remains centered at `x=22`, spanning `theta=riverCenter(22) ± .46` with three open arches; its deck, voussoir rings, barrel surfaces, filled spandrels, full-height piers and widened footing blocks form one structural assembly. Arch spring points overlap the supports, and the spandrel fill follows the extrados up to the deck instead of forming detached flags.

Broadleaf crowns use overlapping, varied smooth ellipsoids with analytic transformed normals and angled boughs joined to tapered trunks; pine silhouettes and flowering/fruiting varieties are distinct. The orchard uses more irregular tree spacing, crown size and species silhouettes. Cottage variants have complete gabled shells (including both long walls), overhanging thick roofs with raised courses, timber framing, divided windows on the axial and bank elevations, porches, steps and chimneys. Glass panes are offset from their frames to prevent coplanar flicker. Grass, flowers, rocks, reeds, banks and path surfaces are placed in intentional patches. Macro terrain relief and broad color regions are smooth and deterministic across the cylindrical seam.

## Mesh contract and resource bounds

The generator uses the shared 12-float / 48-byte `src/scene-contract.js` layout. Every vertex carries a normalized world-space normal, base color, explicit `MATERIAL` ID, wind weight and deterministic phase. Terrain normals use local surface derivatives; ellipsoid and trunk normals are transformed into world space; individual masonry and architectural faces use geometric face normals. Rooted structures use zero wind while foliage and meadow plants have bounded animation weights.

A bounded Node measurement produced **1,374,522 vertices / 458,174 triangles / 65,977,056 bytes**, generated in **about 1.08 s** on the development machine. That is under the 64 MiB vertex-buffer ceiling. `MeshBuilder` grows a compact typed array, checks the 64 MiB limit *before* entering its growth loop, and throws synchronously for impossible requests. `tests/world.test.mjs` exercises immediate over-budget rejection, deterministic output, finite values, normalized normals, material IDs, wind/phase ranges, terrain envelope and seam behavior. `tests/landmark.test.mjs` checks the arch metadata and mesh budget.

## Verification and limitations

`npm test` passes (23 tests), including the geometry ABI, over-budget regression, and assertions that every arch support has a substantial full-section pier reaching the deck. The field layout and geometry are ready for the coordinated renderer/camera passes. The source geometry is procedural and uses no external assets. The scene remains stylized low-poly rather than photoreal: individual roof tiles, small stone joints, vegetation animation and contact lighting depend on the graphics pass for their final rendered appearance. Native 1440×900 WebGPU review captures of the final geometry are available at `artifacts/visual-v2/environment/09-riverbend-rock-cleared.png`, with aqueduct/orchard views at `artifacts/visual-v2/environment/07-aqueduct-final.png` and `08-orchard-final.png`. The riverbend image confirms the entry is no longer hidden by reeds and the glass panes are separated from their frames; the aqueduct image shows connected spandrels, piers and deck. A remaining limitation is stylized low-poly terrain: even with softened palette transitions, lighting and broad field regions can read as large color bands. Orchard crowns remain clustered stylized forms rather than realistic foliage; the camera/integration/review passes should judge these honestly.
